# Interview Script

**Jira:** SCRUM-7 · **Length:** 30–40 min · **Format:** remote, semi-structured

> Tips for the interviewer: ask about **past behavior** ("tell me about the last time…"), not hypotheticals ("would you use…"). Don't mention Biter's solution until the very end. Let silences sit; people often add the most useful detail after a pause.

---

## 1. Introduction (3 min)

"Thanks for joining. I'm researching how people with dietary needs choose where to eat. There are no right or wrong answers. I'm interested in your real experiences. Is it OK if I record this for my notes? The recording stays private, and I won't use your name."

## 2. Warm-up (5 min)

1. Tell me a bit about yourself and how often you eat out.
2. What dietary restriction do you manage? How long have you been managing it?
3. How has it changed the way you eat out, if at all?

## 3. The last time they ate out (10 min)

4. Tell me about the last time you ate at a restaurant. How did you choose it?
5. Walk me through what you did before going. What did you check, and where?
6. How long did that take?
7. How confident were you that you'd be able to eat safely? What made you feel that way?
8. What happened at the restaurant? Did you need to ask staff anything?

## 4. Trust and information sources (8 min)

9. Where do you usually look for information about whether a restaurant suits your needs?
10. Which of those do you trust most? Why?
11. Tell me about a time the information turned out to be wrong. What happened?
12. Whose recommendation would you trust the most for a new restaurant? Why them?
13. When you look at a menu, what information do you wish it had?

## 5. Impact (5 min)

14. Have you ever avoided a place, or skipped going out, because you weren't sure it was safe?
15. How does your restriction affect eating out with friends, family or work colleagues?
16. If you could change one thing about how you find restaurants, what would it be?

## 6. Wrap-up (3 min)

17. Is there anything I didn't ask that you think is important?
18. Would you be open to testing an early prototype later?

"Thank you, this was really helpful."
